---
title: "Transport overview"
description: "Two planes, transport independence, and required bindings."
---

AWP separates a **control plane** (reliable, ordered, low-bandwidth) from a **data plane** (high-bandwidth, loss-tolerant per channel class).

- The control plane carries JSON-RPC 2.0 messages ([control plane](/spec/transport/control-plane)). Worlds MUST offer at least the **WebSocket binding**. One control connection carries one session (AWP-CTL-007). `[AWP-TRN-001]`
- Optional control-plane bindings: stdio (local child processes), gRPC. Bindings MUST preserve message ordering per direction. `[AWP-TRN-002]`
- The data plane carries binary frames ([data plane](/spec/transport/data-plane)). Endpoints are negotiated at `session.open`; defined bindings: WebSocket (binary messages), WebRTC data channels, gRPC streams, POSIX shared memory (local). `[AWP-TRN-003]`
- A world MAY serve data-plane frames inline over the control-plane socket (`inline` binding) — the default for lockstep sessions and REQUIRED as a fallback when no other binding is mutually supported. `[AWP-TRN-004]`

<Note>
AWP assumes the agent and the world share a network environment — the same machine or a trusted local network (an agent client in a browser tab beside a world simulator, a policy process beside a robot controller). Rendezvous across the public internet is out of scope; deployments that separate the two front the world with their own tunnel or proxy presenting a dialable endpoint.
</Note>
